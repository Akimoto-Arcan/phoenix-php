# Recording Defects

**Defect tracking** is how you record quality problems found during production. Every time a product does not meet quality standards, it should be logged as a defect. This helps the team identify patterns and fix recurring problems.

## How to Log a Defect

1. Go to **Production** in the sidebar.
2. Click **Log Defect** (or find the active run and click the **Defect** button).
3. Fill in the required fields:
   - **Type** — Describe the kind of defect (for example, "surface scratch," "dimensional error," or "color mismatch").
   - **Severity** — Select how serious the defect is (see levels below).
   - **Quantity** — Enter the number of units affected.
   - **Description** — Write a brief explanation of what is wrong. Include details like where on the product the defect appears.
4. Click **Save**.

The defect is now linked to the current production run and will appear in quality reports.

## Severity Levels

Every defect is assigned a severity level that describes how serious it is:

- **Critical** — The product is unsafe or completely unusable. Production may need to stop immediately.
- **Major** — The product does not meet specifications and cannot be shipped to the customer without repair.
- **Minor** — The product has a flaw but still functions correctly. It may be acceptable depending on customer requirements.
- **Cosmetic** — The product works fine but has a visual imperfection, such as a scratch or discoloration.

> Tip: When in doubt, choose a higher severity level. It is better to flag something as major and have it downgraded than to miss a real problem.

## Disposition Options

After a defect is recorded, someone needs to decide what to do with the affected units. This is called the **disposition**:

- **Scrap** — The units are waste and will be discarded. They cannot be fixed.
- **Rework** — The units can be repaired or reprocessed to meet quality standards.
- **Accept** — The defect is minor enough that the units can still be used or shipped as-is.
- **Hold** — The units are set aside until someone with authority decides what to do with them.

Your supervisor or quality inspector typically makes the disposition decision.

## How Defects Affect Quality Rate

The system calculates a **quality rate** for each production run:

**Quality Rate = (Good Quantity / Total Quantity Produced) x 100**

For example, if you produced 500 units and 20 were defective, your quality rate is 96%. This number appears on the Dashboard and in reports so management can track quality trends over time.

> Warning: Never skip recording a defect to make numbers look better. Accurate data is essential for finding and fixing the root cause of quality problems.
