# Logging Downtime

**Downtime** is any period when a production line is not running. Tracking downtime helps your team understand why lines stop and where to focus improvement efforts. Even short stoppages add up over time, so logging them is important.

## How to Log Downtime

1. Go to **Production** in the sidebar.
2. Click **Log Downtime** (or find the line on the Dashboard and click the **Downtime** button).
3. Select the **Line** that was stopped.
4. Choose a **Category** that best describes why the line stopped (see the list below).
5. Enter the **Duration** — how long the line was down, in minutes.
6. Add any **Notes** that explain what happened. Be specific so maintenance or management can understand the issue.
7. Click **Save**.

The downtime event is now recorded and will appear in the line's downtime history and in reports.

## Downtime Categories

When logging downtime, you must choose one of these categories:

- **Mechanical** — A machine broke down or a part failed.
- **Electrical** — An electrical fault, sensor failure, or power issue.
- **Changeover** — The line was stopped to switch from one product to another.
- **Material** — The line ran out of raw materials or was waiting for supplies.
- **Planned** — A scheduled stop, such as a break, meeting, or planned maintenance.
- **Operator** — The line stopped because no operator was available or due to an operator error.
- **Quality** — Production was paused because of a quality problem that needed to be fixed.
- **Other** — Anything that does not fit the categories above. Always add a note explaining what happened.

> Tip: If you are not sure which category to choose, pick the one that seems closest and add a clear note. Your supervisor can re-categorize it later if needed.

## How Downtime Affects Efficiency

Downtime reduces the available production time for a line. The system uses this to calculate a more accurate efficiency number.

For example, if a shift is 480 minutes and a line had 60 minutes of downtime, the available production time is 420 minutes. Efficiency is then calculated based on what was produced during those 420 minutes, not the full 480.

Planned downtime (such as breaks) and unplanned downtime (such as breakdowns) are tracked separately in reports so you can see which type of downtime is costing the most.

> Warning: Do not skip logging short stoppages. A five-minute stop that happens ten times a day adds up to almost an hour of lost production.
