# End of Day -- Closing the Register

## Why You Need to Close the Register

At the end of your shift, you must close your register session. Closing the register:

- Locks the session so no more sales can be added to it.
- Records your ending cash count.
- Calculates whether your cash matches the expected amount.
- Creates a clean break so the next person can start a fresh session.

If you leave your session open, the next person on that register cannot start their own session, and your sales totals may become inaccurate.

## Step by Step: Closing Your Session

### Step 1: Count Your Cash

Before closing in the system, physically count all the cash in your register drawer. Include all bills and coins. Write down the total.

### Step 2: Open the Close Session Screen

1. In the **Point of Sale** module, go to the **Register** tab.
2. Look for the **Close Session** option (this appears when you have an active session open).
3. Click **Close Session**.

### Step 3: Enter the Ending Cash Amount

Type the amount of cash you counted in the **Ending Cash** field. Be as accurate as possible.

You can also add optional **Notes** -- for example, if you know about a discrepancy, you can explain it here.

### Step 4: Confirm and Close

Click the **Close** button. The system will:

1. Calculate your total **cash sales** for the session.
2. Calculate your total **card sales** for the session.
3. Count the total **number of orders** you processed.
4. Close the session.

## What the System Shows You

After closing, you will see a summary with:

- **Cash Sales** -- the total amount collected in cash payments.
- **Card Sales** -- the total amount collected in card payments.
- **Total Sales** -- the combined total of all payments.
- **Order Count** -- how many orders you processed during the session.

## What to Do If There Is a Cash Discrepancy

A **discrepancy** means the cash you counted does not match what the system expected. There are two types:

- **Over** -- you have more cash than expected. This could mean you received a higher payment than recorded, or you made change incorrectly.
- **Short** -- you have less cash than expected. This could mean you gave too much change, forgot to record a cash payment, or there was a cash handling error.

If the discrepancy is small (a few dollars), note it in the **Notes** field when closing the session. If it is significant, report it to your supervisor before closing.

Common reasons for discrepancies:

- Giving the wrong change to a customer.
- Voided or refunded orders that involved cash.
- Discounts applied after the sale was partially processed.
- Miscounting bills during the cash count.

## Where to Find Your Session History

Past sessions are recorded for accountability and reporting. Your supervisor or administrator can view session history, including starting cash, ending cash, total sales, and any discrepancies, through the POS reporting tools.

> Tip: Count your cash carefully and slowly. It is better to take an extra minute counting than to close with an unexplained discrepancy.

> Warning: Never close someone else's session without their knowledge. If you need to use a register that has an open session belonging to someone else, contact your supervisor.
