# Preventive Maintenance Schedules

## What Is Preventive Maintenance?

**Preventive maintenance (PM)** is scheduled maintenance performed on a regular basis to keep equipment running and prevent unexpected breakdowns. Instead of waiting for something to break, you inspect, clean, lubricate, or replace parts on a set schedule.

Examples include changing oil every 500 hours, inspecting belts every week, or calibrating sensors every month.

## Setting Up a PM Schedule

1. Click **Maintenance** in the main menu.
2. Click **PM Schedules**.
3. Click the **New PM Schedule** button.
4. Fill in the following fields:
   - **Title** — A short description of the task (for example, "Weekly Belt Inspection — LM2").
   - **Equipment** — Select the machine this schedule applies to.
   - **Frequency** — How often the task should be done. Options include Daily, Weekly, Biweekly, Monthly, Quarterly, Semi-Annually, and Annually.
   - **Checklist** — A list of steps the technician should follow when performing this PM. Enter each step on a separate line.
5. Click **Save**.

The system will automatically track when each PM task is due based on the frequency you set.

> Tip: Write checklists in clear, simple steps. A good checklist says "Inspect drive belt for cracks or fraying" rather than just "Check belt."

## How the System Tracks When PM Is Due

The system calculates the next due date based on the frequency and the date the PM was last completed. For example, if a monthly PM was last completed on January 15, the next one is due on February 15.

You can see all upcoming and overdue PMs on the **PM Schedules** page. Each entry shows the equipment name, task title, frequency, last completed date, and next due date.

## Overdue PMs

When a PM task passes its due date without being completed, it is flagged as **overdue**. Overdue PMs are **highlighted in red** on the PM Schedules page so they stand out.

Overdue tasks should be addressed as soon as possible. Skipping preventive maintenance increases the risk of equipment failure and costly unplanned repairs.

## Completing a PM Task

1. Go to **PM Schedules**.
2. Find the PM task that is due or overdue.
3. Click on it to open the detail view.
4. Review and complete each step in the **Checklist**.
5. Click **Complete** when all steps are done.

After you complete a PM, the system records the completion date and automatically calculates the next due date based on the frequency.

> Warning: Do not mark a PM as complete unless the work has actually been done. Skipping steps or falsely completing PMs defeats the purpose of preventive maintenance and can lead to equipment failures.
