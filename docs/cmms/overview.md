# Maintenance Management (CMMS) — Overview

## What Is CMMS?

**CMMS** stands for **Computerized Maintenance Management System**. It is a tool that helps you organize, track, and manage all maintenance work in your facility.

Instead of relying on verbal requests, sticky notes, or paper logs, the CMMS gives everyone a shared system to report problems, assign repairs, and track maintenance history.

## Main Areas

The CMMS is divided into five sections. You can access each one from the **Maintenance** menu.

- **Dashboard** — A summary view showing open work orders, overdue preventive maintenance tasks, and equipment status at a glance.
- **Work Orders** — Individual tasks that describe what maintenance work needs to be done, who is assigned to do it, and the current status.
- **Equipment** — A registry of all machines and assets in your facility. Each piece of equipment has its own profile with details and maintenance history.
- **PM Schedules** — Preventive maintenance schedules that repeat on a regular basis (daily, weekly, monthly, etc.) to keep equipment running smoothly and prevent breakdowns.
- **Parts** — An inventory of spare parts and maintenance supplies. You can track quantities, set reorder points, and link parts to work orders.

## Who Uses It

Different people use the CMMS in different ways:

- **Maintenance Team** — Creates, manages, and completes work orders. Tracks parts usage and updates equipment status.
- **Supervisors** — Reviews open work orders, monitors overdue PMs, and assigns tasks to maintenance technicians.
- **Operators** — Can submit maintenance requests when they notice a problem with a machine. Operators do not manage work orders directly but can view the status of their requests.

> Tip: If you are an operator and notice a machine problem, you do not need full CMMS access to report it. Use the **Request Maintenance** button in the top navigation bar to submit a request quickly.
